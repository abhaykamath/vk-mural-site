The first thing most people notice on a temple wall is that the figures are the
wrong colour. Krishna is green. A demon is black. A king is red. None of this is
observation, and none of it is decoration.

## Colour as character

The tradition sorts figures by temperament, and gives each temperament a colour.
The serene and the divine are green. Power, appetite and anger are red. What is
base or destructive is black or white. Once you know this, a wall you have never
seen before becomes readable in seconds: you can tell who is who, and what they
are about to do, before you have identified a single story.

This is why the palette can afford to be so small. It is not trying to describe
what light does to skin. It is labelling.

## What this asks of a painter

It asks you to give up something. You cannot flatter a face, and you cannot use
colour to solve a composition, because the colours are already spoken for. What
you get in return is that every choice you do make carries weight.
